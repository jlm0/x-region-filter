LOCAL CHROME EXTENSION · VERSION 0.4.0 · 25 OCTOBER 2026

A quieter X feed, by region and account age.

Starts with India and Africa blocked. Africa includes its individual countries and subregions. The extension uses X’s public Account based in label. Accounts under three months old are also filtered by default.

Install

  1. Unzip the download into a folder you will keep.
  2. Open chrome://extensions in Chrome (or brave://extensions in Brave).
  3. Turn on Developer mode, choose Load unpacked, and select the folder containing manifest.json.
  4. Open or reload your signed-in X Home feed. Pin X Region Filter from the browser’s extensions menu if desired.
  5. Open the extension to change places or pause filtering. Changes apply without reloading the feed.

What gets hidden

In Home (For you and Following) and List timelines, a match for the post’s author, the original author of a repost, a visible reposter, or any visible quoted author hides the entire item. Thus an unblocked author quoting someone from India is hidden too. Mentions in post text do not trigger filtering. Individual post pages, profiles, search, messages and notifications are left readable.

Account age

The account-age rule hides an item when its author, original author, visible reposter or visible quoted author is less than three calendar months old. Either an age match or a region match is enough. Age uses core.created_at from the same About lookup; there are no additional request types, duplicate age checks or higher request limits.

The rule stops matching at the account’s three-month anniversary, measured in UTC with month-end dates clamped to the last valid day. Posts restore automatically when this boundary passes unless another author or region rule still matches. Missing, invalid or future creation dates do not trigger the age rule. Turn it off independently with Hide accounts under 3 months old in the popup. It also works with an empty region list.

Existing cached country results are preserved. Accounts cached by older versions without a creation date are not rechecked early; age data arrives during their normal refresh. New successful lookups save both region and creation date together.

How to tell it is working

Open the extension while viewing X Home or a List. It shows how many posts are hidden in the currently mounted feed, how many nearby authors are waiting, and the last successfully checked account with its country or region. A matching result identifies either the blocked region or under 3 months old. Hidden counts can decrease as X recycles off-screen rows. They are not a lifetime total. Connection failures and rate-limit pauses appear explicitly.

Hidden items become a small 28-pixel placeholder that identifies the region or account-age rule. X retains its own feed rows, scrolling containers and loading controls. Hidden videos are paused; turning the filter off restores the posts but does not automatically restart video playback.

Region coverage

Regional country membership comes from UN M49, retrieved on 25 October 2026. North America combines Northern America, Central America and the Caribbean. The popup lists the countries included by each selected region. Middle East has no single UN M49 membership, so that label and unrecognized custom labels match exactly. A broad label never proves a particular country: selecting India does not hide an account labeled only Asia.

Performance and failure behavior

Only authors near the visible feed are queued. Repeated authors share a lookup. There is at most one active X lookup across tabs, at least 30 seconds after completion before another starts, and no more than 20 starts in a rolling 15 minutes. These limits belong to this extension. Queue size is capped at 60, the disk cache at 2,000 accounts, and each tab’s account cache at 1,000. Responses containing a region or creation date expire after six hours; responses missing both expire after five minutes.

X’s About endpoint was observed allowing 50 requests in a 15-minute window. Those requests are shared with manual About-page visits. Version 0.3 reads the remaining allowance on successful and failed responses, including native About visits, and stops automatic lookups with a reserve of at least 10 requests or 20% of the reported limit (bounded below the total). X can change this allowance, and other clients can consume it. The popup shows the reported remaining allowance and the next check time when paused.

When capacity is low, cached blocked authors still hide; unfamiliar accounts stay visible. Rapid scrolling can therefore outrun classification. This extension cannot reliably filter every new account immediately under X’s lookup limit. Authentication, response-format or endpoint failures also pause lookups. Avoid repeatedly opening About pages during a reported rate-limit pause; wait for the displayed reset time. Outside a quota pause, a successful native About visit can refresh the lookup identifier.

Privacy and permissions

The extension requests access to x.com, local storage and passive network observation. It reads author identities from feed elements and copies the complete Authorization header of X’s own requests into Chrome’s session-only extension storage. Each lookup runs in the extension’s isolated context inside the X tab, reads the current CSRF cookie immediately before sending, and sends those values only to X. The CSRF value is not saved. No password entry, third-party lookup server, analytics, shared country database or cloud synchronization is used.

The extension never rewrites X’s responses or replaces X’s networking functions. It does not mute or block accounts on X. Uninstalling it restores the ordinary feed. Clearing its country cache removes remembered labels, while X rate-limit cooldowns remain in place.

What this version can and cannot establish

Version 0.4 adds the three-month age rule. Creation dates were confirmed in the live About response before implementation. Age-specific hiding and restoration are verified with controlled browser fixtures; this does not claim a particular young account was present in the sampled live feed.

Version 0.2 fixed authentication but its 20-per-minute limit exhausted the shared About allowance. Version 0.3 corrects that design. A native About request was observed failing with HTTP 429 and zero of 50 requests remaining; after X’s reset, the same page returned HTTP 200, India, and 49 requests remaining. The inspected Home response contained no Account based in fields, so separate account lookups remain necessary. X may change its endpoint or page structure.

Twenty-five controlled browser checks passed for direct posts, reposts, quotes, linkless and nested quoted authors, row reuse, delayed responses, navigation, pause/resume, popup feedback, fresh CSRF, correct Authorization, quota propagation, continued cached filtering during a quota pause, young direct/quoted/reposted authors, independent age toggling, automatic restoration at three months, and preservation of older cache entries. The DOM fixture accelerates queue waits; twenty separate rules/worker checks use a controlled clock to prove the actual 30-second spacing, rolling 15-minute budget, preserved reserve, manual-visit accounting, restart persistence, response-order handling, calendar-month and leap-year boundaries, invalid dates, and one-request caching of both fields. A local HTTPS test checks Chrome’s passive header observation and storage privacy. Version 0.3 was checked live: an India-labeled @imSaichowdary_ post displayed the region-filter placeholder in the viewport, automatic requests were about 30 seconds apart, and a manual About visit still succeeded afterward. Earlier testing also verified pause/resume restoration. Quote and repost cases remain fixture-tested; this does not establish every feed layout or sustained reliability across future X changes.

A controlled Chromium 156 stress test rendered 12,000 fixture posts while keeping 40 rows mounted: 4,000 with filtering off and two runs of 4,000 with it on. The fixture includes cached region matches, age matches, and allowed accounts. The 95th-percentile frame interval was 16.9 ms off, 16.9 ms on, and 16.8 ms on the repeat. There were no observed main-thread tasks over 50 ms, no additional requests for cached authors, and no accumulating event listeners. These are measurements of a small synthetic feed on this machine, not performance guarantees for X’s full application.

Countries are X’s account-level labels, not a person’s nationality or precise current location. Authors missing both usable fields and unrendered quoted authors cannot be filtered. Deep quote chains are checked only as far as X actually renders author headers. The first appearance of an uncached account can remain visible while waiting for a lookup. A rapid stream of new authors will exceed the lookup budget; the feed keeps working while filtering catches up.
